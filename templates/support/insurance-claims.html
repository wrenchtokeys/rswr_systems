1

Flag the job as insurance

On the job form, tick Insurance claim and fill in whatever you have — insurer, claim number, deductible. When that job is invoiced, the claim is opened for you on the invoice with those details already in it. Forgot to tick it? The invoice page offers to start a claim with one tap.

2

Expected, received, short — you never type them

The claim works out what the insurer should pay (the invoice total minus the deductible, or the amount they authorized if you entered one), adds up what has actually arrived, and shows the difference as Short. The customer's deductible is recorded like any other payment — it counts toward the invoice, never toward the insurer.

3

Record the insurer's check

When the EOB and check arrive, open the claim and use Record insurer payment — amount, how it arrived, the check or EOB number. The status follows the money on its own: paid in full, or short by exactly the amount they trimmed.

4

Close it when you're done arguing

If the insurer won't pay the rest, Close claim writes off what's still short. It stays on record; it just stops counting as money you're owed. Changed your mind, or another check turns up? Reopen and record it.

{% icon 'lightbulb' class="text-amber-500 mt-0.5" %}

Who sees this: owners and managers only. Claims never appear in the customer portal — your customer sees their invoice and what they owe, nothing about the insurer.
